## Criminal cash laundered through third-party credit card repayments and split money orders

**Audience:** banks, building societies and payment firms in the UK.

### Summary

Organised crime groups are placing cash from drug supply and fraud into the
banking system through small, cash-intensive businesses such as car washes,
convenience stores, vape shops and hand car valets. The business account is
then used to pay off credit cards held by people who have no genuine link to
the business. The cardholders, often recruited as "money mules", spend the
available credit on goods that are later sold on, completing the laundering
cycle.

A second, related method uses money orders. Criminals buy several money
orders at a time, each kept small so that the combined purchase stays just
below the issuer's identity check limit, and pay them into a business account
on the same day.

### Red flags

1. A business account makes repayments to the credit cards of many different
   individuals, typically ten or more cardholders over a three-month period,
   where there is no obvious business reason such as payroll or expenses.
2. The repayments are usually modest, between about £250 and £2,500 each,
   and the same cardholders may be paid several times.
3. A business pays in several money orders on the same day whose combined
   value is just below £2,500. This is repeated on different days, over
   weeks or months.
4. Credit card repayments follow shortly after cash or money order deposits
   into the same account.

### Action

Firms should review whether their transaction monitoring would detect these
behaviours and, where suspicion is formed, submit a Suspicious Activity Report
to the UK Financial Intelligence Unit.
